## The pattern in the errors — weekly volumes were systematically overstated

Figures inferred from head-per-day quotes, distribution footprint or trade press repeatedly ran **above the plant's own USDA volume category ceiling**. The categories are banded head per 360 days and convert to hard weekly ceilings:

| FSIS `slaughter_volume_category` | Head / 360 days | Hard weekly ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | fewer than 1,000 | under ~19 / wk |
| 2 | 1,000 – 9,999 | ~19 – 192 / wk |
| 3 | 10,000 – 99,999 | ~192 – 1,917 / wk |
| 4 | 100,000 or more | ~1,917 / wk and up |

A category is a **ceiling and a floor, not an estimate** — a category-3 plant might be killing 200/wk or 1,900/wk. Use it to *falsify* a claimed figure, never to replace one. On that test the following failed: **Larry's Sausage** (claimed 200–800/wk, capped at ~192), **Acre Station** (claimed ~400/wk, capped at ~192), **Midwest Research Swine** (claimed ~385/wk, capped at ~192), **Fayette Packing** (called "the largest of the TN group", actually under ~19/wk), **Bass Farms** (described as a pure kill floor, actually under ~19/wk).

## 5. STRATEGIC READ AND RECOMMENDATION

### The core problem, stated plainly

Your volume band is not a preference — it is a description of a market segment that has been hollowed out. Above it sit ~30 industrial plants at 5,000–35,000 head/day. Below it sit several hundred very-small multi-species custom shops doing tens of hogs a week. **Between them, by the industry's own capacity census, sit roughly 17 plants nationally — of which 8 are cull-sow plants.** If your models require market-weight hogs, the addressable national pool is about nine plants, and only one of them is in the four states studied. Meanwhile your *cultural* criterion — the Nahunta standard — correlates strongly with the plants that are **too small**, and your *export* criterion correlates strongly with the plants that are **too big or too corporate**. There is no single facility in this study that satisfies volume, culture, federal inspection, EU listing and organ capability simultaneously. **Do not keep searching for one.**

### Recommendation: a three-lane portfolio, not a single plant

**LANE 1 — DOMESTIC ANCHOR: Nahunta Slaughter House (P-80A), deepened immediately.**
This is where you should spend your first and best effort, and the reasoning has changed since the earlier pass. Nahunta is not merely culturally aligned; it **already operates a research-specimen channel under a formal NCDA&CS mechanism, and already pulls viscera off the kill floor intact for retail sale.** That combination does not exist anywhere else in the study at any price. Its state-only grant caps it at US domestic supply — which is fine, because your domestic surgical-training and research market does not require EU listing. Structure it as **Operating Model A** (your harvesters, your protocol, rented line and cooler space), which fits a state-inspected plant far better than Model B and keeps your tissue outside the food chain and outside the intrastate restriction argument. In parallel, raise Talmadge-Aiken conversion for the P-80A entity — NCDA&CS staff already inspect TA plants, so it is the same inspectors — but present it internally as a **real federal grant-of-inspection project with HACCP validation, sanitation SOPs and facility standards, and a real timeline and cost.** It is not a paperwork flip, and whether FSIS is currently accepting new TA establishments in NC is itself unknown.

**LANE 2 — EU-ELIGIBLE VOLUME: a Talmadge-Aiken swine-only NC plant, chosen from three.**
For anything Europe-bound you need slaughter under federal grant, full stop, because Category 3 status depends on ante-/post-mortem inspection. The three realistic candidates are all in NC, all swine-only, all TA, all within a two-hour radius of each other and of Nahunta: **Martin's Pork Products (TA-6720, Falcon)**, **Larry's Sausage (TA-8305, Fayetteville)** and **Bass Farms (TA-3521, Spring Hope)**. Martin's is the incumbent, is confirmed active on a regulator document ten days old, and is now plausibly in band. Larry's is the closest cultural analogue to Nahunta that also has export-eligible inspection. Bass Farms is the cleanest Model A structure in the state — a swine-only kill floor with no processing attached. **Call all three inside one week and let actual weekly kill numbers pick the winner.** Note honestly that none of the three has been adversarially verified to the standard applied to Nahunta and Martin's, so budget one verification pass on whichever wins.

**LANE 3 — ORGAN CAPABILITY AND EU PATH: buy the capability rather than build it, at first.**
**Smithfield BioScience** is the only verified organ programme in the study — aortas, heart valves, pericardium, peritoneum, mucosa, pancreas, pituitary, thyroid, tendons — with stated full farm-to-processing traceability and an existing EU pharmaceutical trade in heparin API. Its existing European regulatory footprint is the single most valuable thing any counterparty in this study could bring you. Approach it, but **approach it eyes-open**: it may regard a porcine-tissue-model company as a competitor rather than a customer, its stunning systems are a different cultural universe from Nahunta, and everything about its APHIS/EU status is currently assumed rather than verified. Treat Lane 3 as *both* a supply option *and* competitive intelligence.

### On multi-plant aggregation

Yes — but as a **geographic cluster, not a scattered network.** The eastern NC / Wilson County concentration (Nahunta, Bass Farms, Custom Quality Packers, George Flowers, Acre Station, Riverside Meats) puts several hog-capable plants inside a short radius. A mobile harvest team that runs Nahunta Monday–Tuesday and a cluster plant Wednesday–Thursday solves at 600–800 hogs/week what no single sub-band plant solves alone, without a second set of relationships in another state. **Aggregation only works if the plants are close enough to share one crew** — which is precisely why the eastern NC cluster is worth more than an equivalent head count spread across TN, VA and SC.

### On buy-direct

Keep it as the hedge, not the plan. Sourcing finished tissue from a biologics harvester is the fastest route to EU-eligible material and requires no plant relationship at all — but it cedes margin, cedes control of the harvest protocol that is presumably your product differentiation, and hands your volume forecast to a potential competitor. Use it to service early European orders while Lanes 1 and 2 mature; do not build the company on it.

### The three highest-ROI next actions, in order

1. **Write the anatomical spec** — live weight range, parity, per block type. It is a one-day internal exercise and it determines whether five sow plants stay on the list or come off it.
2. **Email APHIS VS** (vs.export.product.facilities@usda.gov) on the ABP/Cat 3 pathway. One email resolves the question that currently governs your entire plant selection: whether federal inspection is a hard prerequisite for EU-bound research tissue.
3. **Interview a mucosa or casing collector — Scientific Protein Laboratories (Waunakee WI) or DeWied International (San Antonio TX).** They already run your Operating Model A at industrial scale, and their collection networks are the map you have been trying to build from directories: **which US plants let a third-party crew onto the line, at what price, in what position, under whose HACCP.** This is the single most under-exploited finding in the study, and it was sitting in the file as a dismissed REFERENCE entry.
