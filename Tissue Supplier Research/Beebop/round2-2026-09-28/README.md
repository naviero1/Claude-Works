Distances are estimates from City Hall, not the actual crew base.
2. **Historical inspection volume is useful evidence, but not a hard capacity ceiling.** The Food Safety and Inspection Service's [March 24, 2026 notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/24/2026-05746/revising-establishment-size-definitions) confirms that the numbered slaughter categories cover aggregate heads during the previous 360 days. They do not establish current weekly pig kills, spare capacity or the number of suitable pigs. This also narrows Beebop's earlier criticism: the category boundaries themselves are real. A category-3 boundary converts to about 1,944 total heads per average week, using 7/360, rather than 1,917. That remains a historical aggregate, not capacity.
3. **The live-weight mismatch is also resolved in Claude's current parameters.** The update adopts 140–220 pounds live and lowers sow-only anatomy scores. Plant-level weight distributions remain unknown. Piedmont's published 150-pound minimum overlaps part of the target range. The preferred 2,000–5,000 pigs/week is now explicit in both datasets. Neither review establishes a fully qualified new plant at that scale; a categorical claim that none can exist is not supported by this pass.
4. **Farmington deserves an individual record and an early qualification call.** Claude already mentioned it inside row 74; Beebop's prior 30 omitted it. The [September 18 state directory](https://www.ncagr.gov/divisions/meat-poultry-inspection/wplants/download?attachment=) lists swine slaughter under P-381. Indexed [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/FarmingtonMeat/) and [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/farmington_meat_processing/) descriptions also mention pig processing. Address and telephone match. Distance: approximately 102.6 road / 91.7 radial miles. Weekly pigs and research capability remain unknown.
5. **Two Virginia leads were not named in the reviewed Claude roster:** Easternview Farms and The Butcher's Block in Dry Fork. Easternview's [own site](https://easternviewfarmsllc.com/) advertises pork processing, animal sourcing and establishment M1908. The Butcher's Block is supported by a [September federal-directory mirror](https://localmeat.org/processors/virginia/the-butchers-block-dry-fork-m763) and an independent [Virginia processor list](https://www.vabeef.org/Media/VABeef/Docs/virginia-processors-2024.pdf). Their road distances are approximately 76.0 and 74.0 miles. Mirror data suggest small historical scale; neither is demonstrated to replace Martin's volume.
6. **Neese Burlington moves to unresolved status.** Claude records M8341 inactive on July 2, 2026. The secondary directory used previously by Beebop still presents it as active. A [June acquisition report](https://www.meatpoultry.com/articles/33655-neeses-country-sausage-to-return-under-new-ownership) corroborates a business transition, but does not establish that the Burlington kill floor is operating. Branded sausage returning to shops does not establish local slaughter availability.
7. **The Godwin closure conclusion is not established by the cited Iowa record.** Claude's row 122 refers to inactive establishment M760A in Altoona, Iowa. That cannot independently establish the status of 1600 Martin Road, Godwin. Conversely, a carrier or trade-association listing is not an active slaughter grant. Godwin remains on hold, with no reactivation claim.
