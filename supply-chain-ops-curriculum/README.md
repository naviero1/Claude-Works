# Supply Chain & Operations — A Self-Designed Career Curriculum

A personal curriculum for Oscar Penny: everything under the Supply Chain & Operations Management umbrella — Supplier Engineering, Procurement, Supply Chain Engineering & Planning, Warehouse Engineering & Logistics, Industrial Engineering, Data Analytics & AI, Finance & Cost Analytics, Business Analysis & Process Engineering, Economics, Risk Management & Resilience, and Business Strategy & Leadership — mapped, graded against 20 years of resume evidence, and sequenced into a 4-year plan built for a Supplier Engineer at an ex-vivo models company (outside ISO 13485), without a bachelor's degree, learning while delivering.

## How this was built

Eleven domain-expert analyses (one per discipline), each grading every topic against cited resume evidence (`solid` / `strengthen` / `new`), were integrated into one staged sequence, cross-checked by a peripheral-topics scout, and then attacked by an adversarial completeness critic. The critic's 8 gaps (tissue law by name, customer-audit hosting, biosafety training, contract triage urgency, storage-failure risk, procurement ethics for a sole buyer, supplier-IP/AI data handling, and pricing the degree-completion option) and 10 corrections (inflated grades, credential-eligibility fine print, an overloaded stage, timeline realism) are folded into the final documents.

## The one-paragraph thesis

The common denominator of the career is turning messy operational reality into measured, governed systems — standards, scorecards, dashboards, operating rhythms — that executives fund. The current role asks for that a fourth time, pointed outward at a fragile, sole-source, perishable biological supply base with no regulatory skeleton provided. Designing that skeleton is the curriculum: it cures the "all over the place" feeling, produces the portfolio that substitutes for the missing degree, and builds a defensible niche (biological/cold-chain supplier engineering) almost nobody else holds.
