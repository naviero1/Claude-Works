# SNP Inc. partnership — brainstorm before the slides

*Drafted 2026-09-28. Working document; nothing here is decided or on a slide yet.*

## 1. What the meeting is for (as stated)

- Align strategies.
- Share our forecast; show how volume keeps growing and how we keep delivering.
- Align SNP with our mission.
- Intent: get closer to SNP as partners.

Everything below is "what else" — organised by the direction of value, because a partnership meeting has to give before it asks.

## 2. Give first — what SNP gets from us

| Idea | Why it matters to a small specialist | Effort / cost |
|---|---|---|
| Rolling 12-month forecast, refreshed monthly, plus a 3-year outlook at +10%/yr (150 → ~220 drums/yr) | Lets them plan resin buys and kettle time; growth is the single most attractive thing a customer can show | Hours |
| A multi-year commitment (letter of intent or supply agreement with a minimum) | Converts us from "an order every week" into a planned account they can invest against | Legal time; needs leadership sign-off |
| Level cadence and order discipline: a standing blanket order with weekly releases, no surprise gaps | Removes idle-kettle risk; the opposite of what a second source would do to them | Ops change only |
| Payment terms as a lever (prompt or shortened terms) | Cash matters more to a ~$4M company than to us | Finance approval |
| Reference customer / case study; a plant tour so they see how their product is used | Pride and stickiness; specialists rarely see the end product | Half a day |
| Joint hydrogel roadmap in writing (the brainstorming already scheduled becomes a standing program) | Gives their technical people a reason to prioritise us | R&D time |
| Share our test data back (viscosity trends, shelf-life findings, the viscometer method) | They learn; it also harmonises how we both measure | Hours |

## 3. Learn — what we need to know, framed as continuity, not distrust

Ask these as **supply-continuity requirements**, preferably as Supplier Quality Agreement (SQA) clauses:

- Capacity headroom at Year-5 volume (~99k lb/yr, ~1,900 lb/week): kettles, shifts, drum handling.
- Second line / second site plan; what happens if the PVA kettle is down for a month.
- Key-person cover for the PVA cook and the lab.
- Ownership continuity and financial resilience — under mutual non-disclosure agreement (NDA).
- Resin supply: which producer and grade (Sekisui Selvol / Kuraray Poval or other), their safety stock, willingness to hold 12–26 weeks of dry resin on consignment for us (~$2–6k tied up).
- Advance change notification (resin grade, process, site, ownership) and right to audit.
- Their shelf-life data and a joint 21/30-day study (viscosity build vs. microbial — the limiter is unknown).
- Cleaning / changeover procedure for the PVA kettle (shared kettle?).
- A resin-indexed price formula instead of ad hoc increases — predictable for both sides.

## 4. Grow — more business together (the hydrogel idea)

**The hypothesis.** SNP also makes **sodium alginate** and **guar gum** solutions. If other projects can use those hydrogels, we buy more lines from the same specialist, our share of SNP's revenue rises from ~1% toward something they notice, and the relationship gets stronger in a way that does not depend on PVA volume alone.

**Why it makes sense (supplier-preferencing logic).** Small specialists allocate goodwill, capacity and engineering time to customers they find attractive: growth prospects, trust, long-term orientation, and being a meaningful share of their business (Pulles, Schiele et al. 2016). Buying a second and third line from SNP does all four at once. It also raises SNP's *cost of losing us*, which is the honest way to secure priority without a second source.

**Chemistry check (to confirm with R&D).**

| Material | Charge | Typical use concentration in solution | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) — what we buy today | Non-ionic, synthetic | ~11% solids here | Gels by freeze–thaw (cryogel) or chemical cross-linking; the 90–95 °C dissolution cook is the hard step |
| Sodium alginate | **Ionic** (anionic polysaccharide, from brown seaweed) | ~1–4% w/w | Gels instantly with calcium ions; much lower solids than PVA; viscosity very grade-dependent |
| Guar gum | **Non-ionic** (galactomannan polysaccharide) | ~0.5–2% w/w | Very high viscosity at low concentration; can be cross-linked with borate; hydrates cold |

So the recollection is right: alginate is the ionic one, guar is non-ionic, and both run at far lower solids than our 11% PVA. Spelling: *sodium alginate*, *guar gum*.

**Caveats.** Each new material is a qualification event (spec, incoming test, change assessment). Do not buy what a project does not need; the value is real only if R&D has a use. Ask SNP what else they make and what they would like to make — a partnership meeting is the place to hear it.

## 5. Integrate — operate as one system

- Shared Certificate of Analysis (CoA) data and trending (statistical process control on solids, viscosity, pH) — both sides see the same chart.
- One viscometer method (spindle, rpm, temperature) written into the spec and cross-checked on split samples.
- Consignment or vendor-managed resin buffer.
- Quarterly business review (QBR) with a two-way scorecard: they rate us as a customer too (forecast accuracy, payment, order stability).
- Named escalation contacts on both sides; a one-page "what to do if" for a missed lot.

## 6. Govern — make it durable

- Update the SQA with the continuity clauses above.
- A one-page partnership charter: shared goals, what each side gives, review cadence.
- Annual joint planning session tied to our forecast refresh.

## 7. Share risk — the advanced options (mention only if the conversation is warm)

- We fund the resin buffer and the shelf-life study.
- Joint disaster-recovery plan for the PVA line.
- If they need to add a kettle to grow with us: volume commitment in exchange for capacity, or tooling/capex support (rare at our scale; keep for later).

## 8. What not to do in the room

- Do not name PCI Manufacturing or show its quote. "Our continuity file includes a qualified alternate cooker" is enough — and it is the same discipline we are asking SNP to document.
- Do not let the continuity questions sound like a loss of confidence; sequence them after the give.
- Do not over-promise volume or exclusivity that Legal has not approved.
- Do not announce any change to their volume after the meeting; if the strategy were A, the volume conversation belongs *in* the meeting.

## 9. Questions to answer before anything goes on a slide

1. Which projects could use an ionic or a non-ionic hydrogel, and at what solids and viscosity? (R&D)
2. SNP's approximate revenue and product lines — what share are we today, what could we be? (Oscar / SNP)
3. Who attends from SNP: owner, operations, lab? (shapes the agenda)
4. What can we commit to in writing: forecast sharing yes; multi-year minimum needs whose approval? (Legal / leadership)
5. What does the hydrogel brainstorming session already scheduled cover — can it become the "joint roadmap" line?

## 10. Candidate slide structure (for later, once 1–5 are answered)

1. Where we are: growth, delivery record, mission (the give).
2. Where we are going: forecast, roadmap, joint hydrogel program.
3. How we make it durable: continuity as a shared quality-system discipline (the ask).
4. Growing together: the other hydrogel lines, if R&D confirms a use.
