The opportunity

Thirty-nine FDA-regulated companies across Raleigh–Durham, Greater Boston, and San Diego are, right now, in a moment that creates demand for mock inspections, pre-approval-inspection (PAI) readiness, GxP audits, computer-system validation, or QA resourcing. Every prospect below is tied to a specific, dated event from the 2024–2026 window — not a generic profile.

We sorted them into three bands by how urgent the trigger is. Hot means active FDA enforcement or a public quality/safety crisis — a Warning Letter, a Form 483, a Class I recall, or a Complete Response Letter citing CMC. These carry the sharpest, fastest demand: the company needs remediation and a mock re-inspection now, and usually a QA-resourcing surge alongside it. Warm means a company is approaching or has just passed a regulatory milestone — a first approval, a first BLA, a PDUFA date, or a new device clearance — which opens a PAI-readiness window. Developing means new facilities, expansions, tech transfers, QA hiring surges, or M&A integration — QMS build-out work on a longer sales cycle.

How to match services to the prospect

  • Any 483 / Warning-Letter / recall target → remediation gap audit + mock re-inspection (GMP for drug/biologics, QMSR/ISO 13485 for devices), plus QA staff augmentation to clear the CAPA/deviation backlog.
  • Drug / biologics / CDMO → GMP mock audit + PAI readiness + CSV; add GDP for distribution, GLP if they run labs, and GCP/GVP if they run clinical or pharmacovigilance.
  • Medical device / diagnostics → lead with QMSR / ISO 13485 (the QSR→QMSR transition, effective 2 Feb 2026, is a live urgency hook) + design-control review + CSV for SaMD/algorithm scope.
  • New facility / expansion / greenfield → QMS build-out + validation + PAI readiness; these are longer cycles but larger, multi-year engagements.

The Research Triangle — RTP plus Durham, Raleigh, Morrisville, Cary, Holly Springs, Clayton and Sanford — is one of the largest U.S. biomanufacturing clusters: big biologics/vaccine/injectable plants (Novo Nordisk, Amgen, Biogen, CSL Seqirus), a dense CDMO base (Fujifilm, KBI), a cell-and-gene-therapy layer (CARsgen, Novartis TriCenter), and a growing device and bioanalytical-lab tier. That mix makes it an unusually target-rich territory for mock-audit and QA-resourcing work.

Methodology & sources

Signals were sourced from FDA enforcement pages (Warning Letters, recalls, safety actions), company press releases and investor-relations disclosures, trade press (FiercePharma/FierceBiotech, Endpoints, BioSpace, MedTech Dive) and regional outlets (Triangle / Boston / San Diego business journals, NCBiotech). Every “why-now” is tied to a dated primary or trade-press source, linked on each card and in the accompanying spreadsheet. Companies whose signals could not be verified — or whose sites had de-registered or closed (e.g. Central Admixture Pharmacy, National Resilience's Massachusetts sites) — were dropped.

Contacts. Each prospect card lists named quality / regulatory / CMC leaders (or the deciding executive at smaller firms), tagged by confidence — Confirmed from a company site, SEC filing, or dated press release; Likely from LinkedIn or a secondary aggregator; Dated where the source is 2+ years old and the person may have moved. Where no individual is public, the spreadsheet's Contacts tab gives the target title plus a LinkedIn/Google search string. No names were invented, and no personal emails or phone numbers were collected — people change roles, so re-verify Likely/Dated names on LinkedIn immediately before reaching out.
