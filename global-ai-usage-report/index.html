01 · The big picture

A billion-user habit, formed in three years

Generative AI reached 53% population adoption within three years of launch — faster than the personal computer or the internet did. ChatGPT alone hit 700M+ weekly users by July 2025, roughly 10% of all adults on Earth, sending 18 billion messages a week.

Personal use is overtaking work use

Share of ChatGPT daily messages — and the explosion in volume behind it.

Daily messages grew from 451M to 2,627M in one year (5.8×). Non-work share rose 53% → 73%. Source: OpenAI/NBER, Table 1.

Adoption is racing ahead of every prior technology

  • 53% population adoption of genAI within 3 years (Stanford HAI 2026).
  • 16.3% of the world's working-age population used a genAI tool in H2 2025, up from 15.1% in H1 (Microsoft).
  • 1B+ people use LLM/genAI platforms each month (late 2025).
  • 88% of organizations and 80% of university students now use genAI (Stanford HAI).

Weekly genAI use nearly doubled in a single year — 18% → 34% across six surveyed countries.

Source: Reuters Institute, Gen AI & News 2025 (AR, DK, FR, JP, UK, US).

03 · Modalities

Despite the hype, it's overwhelmingly text

On the world's most-used assistant, image, code, voice and data work are small slices. Multimedia only crossed ~7% after image generation launched in April 2025. Voice and video remain too small to be separately reported.

ChatGPT usage by modality / task consumer

"Text" aggregates writing, information-seeking and practical guidance. Source: OpenAI/NBER, through Jul 2025.

The coding paradox

"How people use AI" has opposite answers depending on the platform's user base.

Coding is just 4.2% of consumer ChatGPT — but 34–46% of Claude. Claude's audience skews heavily to developers, so no single platform represents "how everyone uses AI."
Source: OpenAI (ChatGPT) & Anthropic Economic Index (Claude).
04 · Prompts & tasks

What people actually ask for

Three topics — practical guidance, seeking information, and writing — make up about 77% of all ChatGPT conversations. People mostly ask rather than make; when they make things at work, it's overwhelmingly writing.

Top conversation topics shift over time

% of conversations — Jul 2024 vs mid-2025.

Jul 2024 mid-2025
"Writing" fell 36% → 24% as "Seeking Information" rose 14% → 24% — ChatGPT becoming a search/answer engine. Source: OpenAI/NBER.

Message intent: asking > doing > expressing

  • Writing is the #1 work task — 40% of all work messages.
  • ~10% of all messages are tutoring / teaching requests.
  • At work, 56% of messages are "Doing," ~¾ of which is writing.
Source: OpenAI/NBER message-intent rubric.

Claude tells a different story: technical work leads

Claude.ai task mix by occupational category (% of conversations).

The single most common task is "modifying software to correct errors" (6% of Claude.ai, ~10% of API). Source: Anthropic Economic Index 2025–2026.
05 · Class & the digital divide

AI adoption tracks income — closely

Usage rises almost in lockstep with wealth. A 1% higher GDP per capita is associated with about 0.7% higher AI usage per capita. The result is a sharp Global North–South divide, even as poorer countries grow fastest.

Per-capita usage, indexed by country

Anthropic AI Usage Index — 1.0 = a country's "fair share" for its population.

Israel uses Claude at 7× its population share; Nigeria at 0.2×. Source: Anthropic Economic Index, Sept 2025.

A widening North–South gap

24.7%

of the Global North's working-age population used AI tools in H2 2025 — far above the Global South.

  • Income → usage: +1% GDP per capita ≈ +0.7% Claude usage per capita.
  • Fastest growth is in low-income countries — but from the lowest base.
  • What they use it for differs: coursework dominates in lower-income countries; work & personal use in richer ones.
  • Free vs paid: most consumer usage worldwide runs on free tiers; premium subscriptions concentrate in high-income markets. (Precise conversion rates are not publicly disclosed.)
Source: Microsoft AI Diffusion Report H2 2025; Anthropic Economic Index.
06 · Occupation & use-case

From students to engineers to enterprises

The same tool serves wildly different jobs. Developers lean on Claude for code; office workers use ChatGPT to write; students use AI for coursework; enterprises wire it into APIs to automate whole tasks.

Why people open Claude

Consumer Claude.ai: 46% work · 35% personal · 19% coursework. The enterprise API is 74% work-related. Source: Anthropic, Jan 2026.

Consumer vs enterprise: collaborate vs automate

Share of usage that fully automates a task (vs augmenting a human).

Enterprises buy AI to do the task (77% automation on API); consumers use it more as a collaborator (~50%). Source: Anthropic Economic Index, Sept 2025.

Use-case signals across occupations

  • Developers: coding is 34–37% of Claude conversations — the single largest category.
  • Office / knowledge workers: writing = 40% of ChatGPT work messages.
  • Students & educators: ~10% of all ChatGPT messages are tutoring/teaching; 80% of university students use genAI.
  • Coursework = 19% of all Claude.ai usage.
  • Asia-Pacific employees: 78% report weekly AI use — above the global average.
  • Scientists: ~7% of Claude.ai conversations are scientific tasks.
Sources: Anthropic Economic Index; OpenAI/NBER; BCG Asia-Pacific (Jul 2025, 4,500+ employees, 9 markets); Stanford HAI 2026.

08 · Geography

Who drives the volume — and who's catching up

The United States generates the most AI usage in absolute terms, but ranks only third per capita. India and Brazil are now top-three traffic sources for ChatGPT, and India leads Asia-Pacific on workplace adoption.

Share of ChatGPT web traffic by country Aug 2025

US 15.1% · India 9.3% · Brazil 5.3% · UK 4.3% · Indonesia 3.7%. Source: Similarweb.

Absolute Claude usage share

India leads Asia-Pacific workplace adoption at 92%; Japan trails at 51%.

Sources: Anthropic Economic Index, Sept 2025; BCG Asia-Pacific 2025.
Reconciling the two pictures: the US leads on absolute volume and per-capita intensity in mature markets, while India, Brazil, Indonesia and Nigeria post the fastest growth — adoption in the lowest-income countries grew several times faster than in the richest by mid-2025.
09 · Caveats & method

How to read these numbers

What we did

  • Decomposed the question into 5 research angles and ran parallel web searches.
  • Fetched 27 sources and extracted 114 falsifiable claims.
  • Put the top 25 through 3-vote adversarial verification; 23 survived (2 were refuted and dropped).
  • Prioritised primary vendor data and named survey houses over blogs.

Limits to keep in mind

  • Snapshots, not trends: figures are dated 2025–2026 and move fast (e.g. India's Claude share was 7.2% in Sept but 5.8% in a Nov 2025 brief).
  • Platform ≠ population: Claude is developer-heavy, ChatGPT is mainstream — neither alone is "how everyone uses AI."
  • Self-reported vendor data covers only each company's own users; gender is name-inferred.
  • Traffic estimates (Similarweb) measure web only, excluding apps and API.
  • Thinly-covered gaps: exact free-vs-paid conversion, voice/video shares, and some occupation-by-occupation breakdowns lack public hard numbers.
