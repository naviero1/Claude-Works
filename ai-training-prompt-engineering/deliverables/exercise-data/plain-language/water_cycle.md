# Topic: the water cycle (self-study variation)

## SOURCE (give this to the assistant)

The water cycle describes the continuous movement of water on, above, and below
Earth's surface. Solar energy drives evaporation from oceans, lakes, and soil;
plants release additional water vapor through transpiration. As moist air rises
and cools, vapor condenses into droplets, forming clouds. When droplets grow
heavy enough they fall as precipitation — rain or snow depending on temperature.
Precipitation either runs off into rivers and eventually the ocean, soaks into
the ground to recharge groundwater, or is stored temporarily as ice and snow.
Residence times vary enormously: water may stay in the atmosphere about nine
days on average, but in glaciers or deep aquifers for centuries or longer. The
cycle conserves total water: it changes form and location but is neither created
nor destroyed in the process.

## SAMPLE OUTPUT (what a good result looks like)

### The main idea
Earth keeps reusing the same water, moving it in a loop between the sea, the
sky, and the land.

### How the loop works
The sun warms water in oceans and lakes and turns some of it into **water
vapor** — water in the form of a gas. Plants add vapor to the air too. High in
the sky, the vapor cools and turns back into tiny droplets that make clouds.
When droplets get heavy, they fall as rain — or snow, when it is cold. That
water then flows to rivers and the sea, soaks into the ground, or is stored as
ice, and the loop begins again.

### The part people miss
Water does not move through the loop at one speed. A drop may spend about nine
days in the air — but centuries locked in a glacier or deep underground.

An analogy: the cycle is like a giant recycling system — nothing is thrown
away, everything is reused. The analogy stops here: real recycling changes
products into new products, while the water cycle keeps the water itself the
same and only changes its **form** (liquid, vapor, ice) and its **place**.

### Check yourself

3. Is new water created in the cycle? *(No — the same water changes form and
