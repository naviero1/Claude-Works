Spending more makes the meal worse

Across sixty dishes, every additional $5 on the bill brings 105 more milligrams of sodium and 1.1g less fibre — and moves the health score by +0.04, which is nothing. Price and health correlate at +0.05. Money is not neutral here. It is mildly harmful.

Stop treating the expensive option as the careful one.

$17.00the price above which nothing is efficient

Every dish over $17 is beaten by a cheaper one

Only four dishes in the whole set are efficient — meaning nothing cheaper is also as healthy. All 30 priced above $17.00 are matched or beaten by something that costs less. Chengdu 7 charges $36.95 for a health score Chopt reaches at $10.79.

Treat $17.00 as a ceiling, not a floor.

+572extra milligrams of salt in the food that tastes best

Flavour is bought with salt, not fat

The dishes that taste best carry +572mg more sodium than the blandest ones — but only +2.0g more saturated fat, which is statistical noise. A quarter of what money appears to buy in flavour is simply salt. Everyone is watching the wrong nutrient.

Watch the sauce, the base and the cure. Stop watching the olive oil.

How this was put together

What was measured

Each entry is one specific dish, ordered one specific way. Seven health measures — sodium, saturated fat and calories, protein, fermented food and plant variety, refined carbohydrate, omega-3, and sugar — plus flavour and cost. Where the instruction says to refuse something, the figures assume you refused it.

Where the numbers come from

Nine dishes are built from figures the restaurant publishes. The rest are estimated from how the dish is constructed, and the full document marks which is which.

What it cannot tell you

No restaurant that cooks to order over fire publishes nutrition data, so every grilled, stewed and steamed dish here is an estimate. When we rebuilt dishes against a restaurant's own figures, eleven of thirteen turned out worse than estimated — so read an estimate as roughly half a point generous.

Not medical advice

This compares restaurant meals with each other. It is not a diagnosis, and the thresholds behind the scores are judgement calls, argued out in the full document.

The illustrations are drawings of dish types, not photographs of any particular restaurant's food. Prices and menus change constantly; every figure here has a shelf life measured in months.
