1. Philosophy in one paragraph

Predict a distribution of scorelines, never a single fake-precise score. Anchor every forecast to the betting market (Kalshi / Polymarket) because market-implied probabilities are the best-calibrated public forecast that exists — but treat the market as crowd belief, not ground truth. Our independent analysis (Dixon-Coles + players + cohesion + mentality + a human expert) earns its keep only at defensible divergences: spots where we can name a structural reason the crowd is mispricing. Bet only those; pass everything else.

2. The layered model

MARKET ANCHOR → de-vigged Kalshi / Polymarket implied probabilities (the prior) + DIXON-COLES → team attack/defense → expected goals λ, μ + FORM & DECAY → recent matches weighted more (time decay ξ); signal vs noise + PLAYERS → disruptor availability & impact; H2H neutralisation + COHESION → collective > sum of parts (settled XI, system, chemistry) + MENTALITY → belief/resilience compresses talent gaps (the "Uruguay principle") + EXPERT → a trusted player's eye on form/fitness/tactics (strong prior) = SCORELINE DISTRIBUTION + ADVANCE PROBABILITY + $100 ALLOCATION

2.1 Market anchor — crowd belief, not truth

De-vig sportsbook/market prices so they sum to 1: fair_i = implied_i / Σ implied_j. Prediction markets are near-fair already. The market is the number to beat, moved off only with a stated reason. It carries biases we exploit: favorite-longshot, recency/narrative, sentiment-inflated popular teams (Brazil, Argentina, England), thin-market noise, and it is slow to price structural factors (rotation, key injuries) until the team sheet drops.

2.2 Dixon-Coles statistical core

Each match = two Poisson processes. Expected goals:

λ (home) = α_home · β_away · γ μ (away) = α_away · β_home α = attack rating · β = defense weakness · γ = home advantage

Two corrections over naïve Poisson:

  • τ low-score correction (ρ): reweights 0-0, 1-0, 0-1, 1-1 — real football has more cagey low-scoring games than independence implies. We use ρ ≈ −0.06.
  • Time decay (ξ): each past match weighted exp(−ξ·Δt) — recent form matters more.

2.3 Advance probability (knockouts)

Markets quote to-advance (incl. ET/penalties), not 90-minute results. We convert:

advance_home = P(win 90') + P(draw) · q q = 0.5 + 0.5·(winFav − winDog)/(winFav + winDog)

The better side wins the shootout/ET slightly more often. This fix alone closed most of the early gap between our numbers and the market on favorites.

2.4 Player / disruptor layer

Rate each likely starter's disruption degree (1–10) — capacity to swing a tight game (Messi/Mbappé 10; Kane/Bellingham/Musiala/Yamal/Haaland 8–9; Pulisic/Cherki/James "relatively" 6–7). Then: (1) availability gate — not playing → 0; (2) form multiplier — last-10 output, opponent-adjusted; (3) H2H neutralisation — net disruption that survives the matchup drives the edge, converted to a ±λ adjustment. In knockouts, individual match-winners weigh more than in league play.

2.5 Cohesion & 2.6 Mentality (the Uruguay principle)

Cohesion = how well a team plays as a unit (settled XI, same-club spine, drilled system) — mainly lowers goals conceded and lifts draw probability (Morocco, Iran, Japan, Cape Verde). Mentality = belief/resilience under pressure; it compresses talent gaps and raises draw/penalty likelihood. Both mean: do not over-price a favorite against a high-belief, organised underdog; widen the distribution. This is a source of irreducible uncertainty — expressed as a flatter distribution, not false precision.

2.7 Expert-in-the-loop & 2.8 Goals by half

A trusted human read (a current/former player) is a first-class input on form, fitness, tactics and mentality — a strong Bayesian prior that can override lagging stats. Goals split ≈ 45% first half / 55% second half; early-goal probability = 1 − e^(−(λ_h+λ_a)·15/90).

2.9 Allocation & staking ($100 per match)

Edge is measured vs the de-vigged market: if our probability equals the market's, edge = 0 and the correct stake is 0 (agreeing with the price just pays the vig). For each candidate bet at price m (odds o = 1/m), our prob p:

edge = p − m EV($1) = p·(o−1) − (1−p) [>0 ⇒ "bargain"] Kelly f = edge/(o−1) (use ¼·f)

We present four allocation styles per game — Max-EV, Max probability-of-profit, Balanced, Low-scoring — because maximising EV and maximising P(profit) rarely point at the same bet. Caps: ≤5% bankroll/day, ≤2.5%/game. The durable edge lives in the to-advance markets on over-priced favorites, not in exact scores or totals (which are ~fairly priced).

3. Workflow — how a match is analysed

  1. Pull the live Kalshi/Polymarket board (to-advance % + exact scores + totals). De-vig.
  2. Build λ, μ from strength/form + reconcile to the market's totals/team-totals.
  3. Adjust for players (availability, disruptors, H2H), cohesion, mentality; fold in the expert read.
  4. Compute the scoreline grid → top scenarios, W/D/W, advance %, O2.5, BTTS, goals by half.
  5. Compare to the market; deviate only with a named structural reason.
  6. Build the four $100 allocations; recommend one by risk profile; log the pick before kickoff.
  7. After the game: grade result, exact score, Brier, and bet P&L; feed lessons back.

5. Key lessons codified

  • Anchor to the market; deviate only with a nameable structural reason (crowd belief ≠ truth).
  • The recurring edge is fading sentiment-inflated favorites in to-advance markets (validated by Morocco & Paraguay).
  • Cohesive/high-mentality underdogs drag favorites to penalties — where shootout keepers (Bono) swing ties; price that in.
  • Max-EV ≠ max-P(profit): always present the allocation menu, size by conviction, cap exposure.
  • Dead-rubber / seeding games skew low-scoring — down-weight Over/BTTS.
  • Grade everything (Brier + P&L) before the next slate; that feedback is the system.
