## 2. What the material is used for  *(5 min)*

- We make **advanced tissue models** — synthetic materials engineered to behave like real human tissue.
- They're used in **device development, validation, and training** — so our teams can test and demonstrate instruments against something that responds like tissue.
- The PVA solution you'd make is the **base material**. It goes through downstream processing on our side (including freeze/thaw cycling) to become the finished model.
- **Why this matters to them:** the mechanical behaviour of the finished model depends directly on how consistently the solution is made. Batch-to-batch variation in solids or viscosity shows up as variation in the model. That's why our spec is tight — it isn't arbitrary.

---

## 3. The product and process  *(15 min — the technical core)*

**What we need made:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Product | Aqueous polyvinyl alcohol solution, super-hydrolyzed, high molecular weight |
| Total solids | 10–12% in water |
| Also in the formula | A small amount of NaCl, pigment, and a biocide (Proxel BD20) |
| **Viscosity** | **Under 2,500 cps** — Brookfield, spindle #3, 10 RPM, 25 °C |
| **The cook** | **Heat to 90–95 °C and HOLD 30–45 min** under agitation until fully dissolved |
| Biocide addition | Added hot, in the final ~10 minutes |
| Cool | To ≤ 40 °C before packaging |
| Filter | 200 micron |
| Package | 55-gallon drums, ~450 lb net |
| Shelf life | **18 days** — made fresh to order, we cannot stockpile |
| Volume | **~1 drum per week, ~50 drums/year**, recurring |
| Model | **Toll — we supply the resin and all raw materials.** You charge a conversion fee. |

**Questions for them:**

1. Confirm you can **reach and hold 90–95 °C for 30–45 minutes**. *(This has eliminated three suppliers already — but your equipment looks well suited.)*
2. Your site lists **5–100 gallon pilot reactors** with steam and hot oil. **Could one of those run this as recurring production?** Our batch is ~50 gallons — it looks like a near-perfect fit.
3. Contact surfaces — is **316L stainless** available? *(Our formula contains salt, and chlorides pit lesser grades.)*
4. Cooling to ≤ 40 °C, and 200-micron filtration — both in-house?
5. Drum filling, and **protect-from-freezing** handling in transit?
6. **CoA every shipment** with: lot #, production date, % total solids, final pH, and Brookfield viscosity **at our stated conditions**?
7. **ISO 9001?** Lot traceability and retained samples?
8. **Minimum batch size** and **MOQ**?
9. **Lead time** from PO to delivery — can you support a weekly cadence?
10. Any **experience dissolving PVA or similar water-soluble polymers**? *(Their textile-sizing heritage suggests yes — worth drawing out.)*

## 4. Commercial  *(15 min)*

### Ask them first — then stop talking

> **"Assuming this all works — what would you charge us per 55-gallon drum, as a conversion fee, with us supplying the materials?"**

Then let them answer. Don't fill the silence with a number.

**Two things to nail down whatever they say:**
- **Unambiguous basis.** Get it **per drum** or **per batch**. If they quote per pound, ask *per pound of what* — finished solution, or dry resin? (A previous supplier's "per pound" quote turned out to be ~10× what we assumed. Don't repeat that.)
- **What's included vs extra** — QC testing, filtration, drum, freight.

### Then our position

- We'd be looking for a conversion fee in the range of **$350–500 per drum**.
- That lands all-in around **$1.00–1.40/lb** once our materials and freight are added — a premium over our current cost that we can justify for a second source.
- **Above roughly $900/drum this stops making commercial sense** for us, and we'd have to look elsewhere.

> *These figures come from our own cost model, not a mandate — adjust to whatever you're actually authorised to commit.*

**Do not mention:** our current supplier's name, or what we pay them today.

**Useful leverage, if the conversation stalls on price:**
- Materials and energy are a small part of this — the resin is roughly $0.15–0.20 per pound of finished solution, and heating a 450 lb batch costs a few dollars. **The cost here is your time and setup, so the honest lever is batch size.** Ask: *would running 2 drums per batch, every other week, meaningfully lower the per-drum price?*
- Under 2,500 cps is a **thin, easily pumped and easily filtered** fluid. This should not be priced as a difficult material.

### Also cover
- **Trial / qualification batch** — do they need one, and what does it cost? Is the fee credited against production?
- **Payment terms**, and whether pricing can be held for a year.
