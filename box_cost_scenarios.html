What we should be paying at 100

An acceptable range needs a defensible definition, so both ends come from evidence already on this page. The floor is the independent converter-direct estimate — below it is implausible for custom work. The ceiling is the dearest comparable box anyone publishes: pay more than that and we are paying above retail for a custom job, which is never defensible. The target is the midpoint.

The medium box is $11.41 over its ceiling — 61% above the most expensive comparable box on the open market. The large box lands $0.07 under its ceiling: inside the acceptable range, but by seven cents, which is not a margin worth defending.

OrCon lands where the range says it should. Their medium box at $14.94 sits in the good zone, between floor and target. Their large box at $30.70 comes in $0.23 below the modelled floor.

Atlas is below the floor on both — $11.15 and $19.83 projected to 100, against floors of $13.99 and $30.93. That is worth saying plainly: the acceptable range I built was too generous. Real competition came in under the floor I modelled from published data, which means the band should now be read as a ceiling test rather than a fair-price estimate.

* Atlas is marked with an asterisk throughout because they will not sell 100. Their figure at that quantity is projected from a 250-unit quote so the three can be compared on one basis — it is not a price anyone can buy.

Bringing both to the ceiling saves $1,141 on the current order (17%). Bringing both to target saves $1,777 (26%). Nearly all of it is the medium box.

What the three scenarios show

On rate

Projected to a common 100 units so all three can be read against each other, a set costs $30.97 from Atlas, $45.64 from OrCon and $69.11 from us. Atlas is 55% below what we pay now.

On the
real order

Their minimum costs less than our current orderstrong

Atlas will not sell fewer than 250. That is fine: 250 of each comes to $6,830, against the $6,911 we spend today on 100 of each. We would pay $81 less and receive 2.5× the boxes. The MOQ is not an obstacle here — it is the reason the price is good.

Why the
gap exists

Same board rate as ours. The difference is setup.

Split each quote into a board rate and a fixed charge and the result is striking: Atlas runs at $188/MSF and we are charged $188/MSF — the same board economics to within a fifth of a percent. But Atlas carries $304 of setup per box type and we carry $2,213. That $1,908 gap, spread over a small order, is the entire difference. OrCon is the mirror image: barely any setup but a much dearer board rate at $342/MSF.

Where each
one wins

Volume decides between OrCon and the other two

OrCon's no-setup structure is built for small runs; Atlas and our incumbent both price board keenly and recover it through setup. Atlas wins at every quantity here because it combines the low board rate with a setup charge 7× smaller than ours. OrCon only becomes competitive if the order stays genuinely tiny — and below 250 they are the only one of the two who will take it.

What is real here, and what is not

Four figures on this page are real quoted prices, all at 100 of each: $30.22 and $38.89 from our incumbent, $14.94 and $30.70 from OrCon. The catalogue range is drawn from five comparable 275 lb test double-wall RSCs — the equivalent grade to our 48ECT — priced at the matching quantity, so it reflects the real spread in the market rather than a single point. Everything else is worked out.

Every figure at 250 and 1,000 is deduced, for both suppliers. Neither quoted those quantities. OrCon's extrapolation is the weaker of the two, because a supplier showing no setup charge at 100 is very likely to discount at volume and the flat line drawn here assumes they will not.
How the deduction works. They come from splitting our two actual prices into a board rate and a fixed charge, then re-spreading the fixed charge over a larger run. Fitting two unknowns to two data points reproduces our real prices exactly by construction — that is arithmetic, not evidence. The direction is not in doubt, because fixed cost provably does not scale with quantity, but the exact figures need confirming. Ask the provider to state their setup charge separately on the next quote — if it lands near $2,213 per box type, this whole page becomes solid ground.
All figures exclude freight, so they compare cleanly. Delivery to Sterling on a 100-of-each order works out at roughly $3.27 per medium box and $6.78 per large. Worth confirming whether our provider's price already includes delivery — if it does, they are better value than shown here.
