What our boxes cost, and what order size does to that

We pay $30.22 for the medium box and $38.89 for the large, at 100 of each. Measured against what comparable boxes actually sell for, the medium one is well outside the range and the large one sits at the top of it — and both change character completely once the order gets bigger.

1,000 each

Well below anything publishedstrong

Medium $10.31 against $15.20–$16.63; large $18.98 against $31.48–$34.45. Our price falls 66% and 51% from the 100-unit level while the published range moves only about 12% — retail discounts very little for volume, and that divergence is the whole argument.

Why

It is an order-size problem, not a supplier problem

Splitting the two prices we actually pay gives a board rate of about $188 per MSF, which is competitive, plus roughly $2,213 per box type of fixed cost. That fixed charge does not care how big the box is, so at 100 units it lands hardest on the smaller one. Our provider's underlying economics are sound; the order is simply too small to carry them.

Watch

One published price sits below the whole range

A 48 × 40 × 36 in box — 5% larger than our large one, same 275 lb double-wall grade — is listed by one retailer at $33.59 in quantities of 20 or more, which works out below every box in our comparison set. A second retailer lists the same box at $45.95. That ~37% spread between sellers of an identical item is a reminder that no single catalogue price is the market price, which is why the range above is drawn as a band rather than a line.

What is real here, and what is not

Two figures on this page are prices we are actually charged: $30.22 and $38.89, at 100 of each. The catalogue range is drawn from five comparable 275 lb test double-wall RSCs — the equivalent grade to our 48ECT — priced at the matching quantity, so it reflects the real spread in the market rather than a single point. Everything else is worked out.

The 250 and 1,000 provider figures are deduced, not quoted. They come from splitting our two actual prices into a board rate and a fixed charge, then re-spreading the fixed charge over a larger run. Fitting two unknowns to two data points reproduces our real prices exactly by construction — that is arithmetic, not evidence. The direction is not in doubt, because fixed cost provably does not scale with quantity, but the exact figures need confirming. Ask the provider to state their setup charge separately on the next quote — if it lands near $2,213 per box type, this whole page becomes solid ground.
All figures exclude freight, so they compare cleanly. Delivery to Sterling on a 100-of-each order works out at roughly $3.27 per medium box and $6.78 per large. Worth confirming whether our provider's price already includes delivery — if it does, they are better value than shown here.
